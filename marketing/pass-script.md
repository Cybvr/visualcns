[TITLE]: KNOW WHO'S IN

[ABOUT]: VisualCNS Pass is visitor sign-in for offices. It replaces the paper visitor book at the front desk. Visitors sign in on a tablet at the desk, and other visitors never see their phone number or who they came to see. The person they're visiting gets an email straight away. Pass prints a visitor badge with the visitor's name, host and time in. A live list shows who is in the building right now, which doubles as a roll call in a fire alarm. Every visit is saved, searchable and exportable as a CSV file for auditors. If the internet drops, the tablet keeps signing people in and catches up when it's back. Pass runs on any tablet with a browser. Free for 30 days, no card needed.

[SCENE SUMMARY]: A 60-second, 9:16 vertical VisualCNS film for Pass, aimed at security, IT and facilities leads. It is made like an infographic. Frames switch hard between white and black. Each idea gets one big word that fills the frame, then a simple diagram of the real Pass workflow, with numbered callouts, leader lines, system log lines and counters. One thick line runs through the whole film. The tone is calm and exact: it sells control, records and speed, not fun.

[SUBJECT & SETTING]:
SUBJECT: Pass itself, shown through simple diagrams and its real screens: the paper book, a top-down office floor plan, the tablet sign-in screen, the host email, the printed badge, the live "In the building" list, the visit history search and the CSV export.
SETTING: No people and no rooms. Flat white frames with black type, and flat black frames with white type. VisualCNS blue is the only colour, used for the line and anything Pass does. Red is used for the paper book risk tags and the fire alarm scene only.

[VISUAL DEVICE]: THE LINE. One thick line runs through every scene, and a small dot rides along it as the visitor.
- In the paper book it is a ruled line with a name on it. It gets scribbled over and tangled.
- When Pass arrives it snaps straight and turns blue.
- From then on it is the visitor's route through a top-down floor plan: front door, then the tablet, then the host, then the "In the building" list.
- In the fire alarm it turns red and runs down the live list.
- At the audit it stretches out into a timeline of saved visits, with one tick per visit.
- At the end it draws an underline under "Know who's in your building."
